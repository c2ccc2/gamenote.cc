Why Organize Game Guides Again?

Finding an answer and finding the answer needed right now are two different things.

2026-09-26

Guide editor: GAME NOTE · Updated 2026-09-27

Finding a game guide is easy these days.

Search for a game name together with a boss, map, collectible or quest, and an answer usually appears quickly. Videos, wikis, forums and community posts offer an ever-growing wealth of information.

But finding an answer and finding the answer needed right now are two different things.

Often, a player simply wants to know:

“Where do I go next from here?”

Yet the search result may be a complete walkthrough that starts at the beginning of the game.

A player missing just one collectible may have to work through dozens of item descriptions to find it.

The map is on one page, the boss on another, and ability unlocks scattered elsewhere. Each source may be perfectly useful on its own, but during play there is still a lot of switching, searching and interpreting to do.

GAME NOTE therefore aims to do more than simply add another guide:

It aims to organize complex game information clearly.

This matters especially in exploration-focused games.

A new ability opens areas that were previously inaccessible.

A collectible that cannot be reached yet may only become available several hours later.

A place that looks nearby on the map may require a long detour to reach.

For games like these, simply arranging everything into Chapter One, Chapter Two and Chapter Three may not make information easy to find.

A guide needs to answer additional questions.

Should an ability be documented in the walkthrough, in the ability list, or both?

Besides marking a collectible’s location, should the guide explain when it becomes obtainable?

Should a map contain every possible detail, or focus on the important nodes?

Should a boss page explain only combat tactics, or also its relationship to the areas before and after it?

These may look like questions about website structure, but they all lead to the same goal:

“Standing here right now, what does the player most need to know?”

GAME NOTE does not want to become a website that merely has a lot of articles.

The number of guides is not what matters.

What matters is that a player who is stuck can open a page without first having to learn how the website works.

Where is the map?

Where should the player go next?

When does this ability become available?

What needs attention in this boss fight?

Which items remain inaccessible for now and can be revisited later?

When this information is easy to find, a guide has truly done the work of organizing it.

Publishing a page does not mean that work is finished.

A route may still need adjustment.

Map markers may be made clearer.

A step may be technically correct and still difficult to understand.

Pages therefore continue to be revised through actual use.

Rather than being the first guide published, GAME NOTE hopes to preserve a game archive that becomes clearer through continued organization.

Perhaps the real value of a guide is not having more information, but saving players a few detours while looking for it.
